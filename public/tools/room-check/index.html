Sound index

How does this room sound?

Use your phone to measure how loud a space is and how long sound hangs in the air. It takes about two minutes.

How to measure
  1. Stand roughly in the middle of the room, away from walls. Hold the phone at chest height with the bottom microphone uncovered.
  2. Fill in the venue details and pick how busy it is right now.
  3. Noise level: keep still and quiet for 30 seconds while the phone listens. Do not talk near the phone.
  4. Echo: tap Record, wait for "Clap now", then give one hard clap with cupped hands (or pop a balloon), about an arm's length from the phone. Stay silent until it finishes. Do this three times.
  5. Echo readings work best when the room is fairly quiet, such as before opening or in a lull.
  6. Save the result. Nothing is uploaded: it stays on this phone until you export or share it.

Phone microphones are not sound level meters. Levels are approximate (often within 5 dB, sometimes worse) unless you set a calibration offset against a real meter. Echo time (RT60) does not depend on calibration and is usually much more reliable.

      Calibration

      Levels are shown as the phone's digital level plus an offset. The default (+120 dB) is a typical value for phones with audio processing switched off; individual phones vary. If you have a sound level meter, run a 30 second measurement next to it and type its reading below.

      Readings are estimates from a phone microphone for guidance, not a certified acoustic survey. Ask Just Acoustics for a proper assessment.
